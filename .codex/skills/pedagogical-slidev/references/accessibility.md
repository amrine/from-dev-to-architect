# TeamPulse Accessibility Guide

Accessibility is part of pedagogical quality. A slide that cannot be read,
navigated, or interpreted by part of the audience does not prove its objective.

## Content and language

- Use direct French and explain acronyms on first meaningful use.
- Keep one idea per slide and avoid dense paragraphs.
- Make headings descriptive rather than generic.
- Do not encode meaning only through color, position, animation, or sound.
- Put the essential conclusion in text, not only in a diagram.

## Visuals

- Preserve the TeamPulse contrast system and verify text against its background.
- Use teal, blue, and amber as semantic accents with labels or icons as support.
- Keep code and diagram text readable at `1280x720` and around `390x844`.
- Avoid small footnotes that carry essential information.
- Give diagrams a reading direction and a textual conclusion.
- Provide meaningful labels for links and external references.

## Motion and interaction

- Use progressive reveals to control complexity, not as decoration.
- Ensure the fully revealed state remains understandable.
- Do not make the learning objective depend on a timed animation.
- Keep keyboard and route navigation coherent when the deck supports it.

## Presenter notes and alternatives

- Notes must not contain secrets or private operational data.
- A visual explanation should have a textual oral explanation.
- An exercise should state what the learner must produce and how success is
  recognized.
- When a diagram is too dense, provide a simpler reading path or split it.

## Review questions

1. Is the objective understandable without perfect color perception?
2. Can a learner identify the important relationship in the diagram?
3. Is text readable on a small viewport?
4. Does motion preserve meaning when skipped or fully revealed?
5. Are links, notes, exercises, and proof steps explicit?
